How SAR works

Synthetic aperture radar (SAR) lets a satellite see the ground in four simple steps.

1

Send a pulse

The satellite sends a short burst of radio waves toward the ground. It makes its own signal, so it works at night. The waves pass through clouds, smoke and rain.

2

The ground answers

Each surface sends back a different amount. Smooth water bounces the pulse away, so very little returns. Crops scatter some of it. Buildings and rock send back a lot.

3

Time and strength are recorded

The satellite times each echo to work out how far away the surface is. It also measures how strong the echo is, which tells it what kind of surface it hit.

4

Many pulses become one image

As the satellite flies on, it sends thousands of pulses and combines the echoes. The result is a sharp image. Compare two passes and you can see what changed.
